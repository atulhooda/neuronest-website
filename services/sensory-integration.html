About this therapy

What it is.

Some children are over- or under-sensitive to what they feel, hear, see, or how they move. They might cover their ears at everyday sounds, avoid messy play, crave spinning and crashing, or barely notice bumps and scrapes. Sensory integration therapy helps their nervous system process this information more comfortably.

Sessions take place in our therapy room with balls, soft blocks, mats, hoops and adjustable lighting, in a planned way that feels like play.

Signs it might help

  • Covers ears at ordinary noises
  • Avoids certain clothes, textures or messy play
  • Constantly seeks movement: spinning, jumping, crashing
  • Very easily overwhelmed, or very hard to calm
  • Seems not to notice touch or pain

Families ask

Good questions.

Is sensory processing difficulty the same as autism?

No. It is common in autistic children, but many children without autism have sensory difficulties too.

What is a sensory diet?

A personalised set of activities spread through the day to help your child stay calm and focused.

Why do you change the lights in the room?

Lighting is one of the tools we use to add or reduce visual input, depending on what your child needs.
