About this therapy

What it is.

Swallowing difficulty (dysphagia) can follow head and neck cancer treatment, a stroke, brain injury, or conditions such as Parkinson's. It can make eating tiring, cause coughing during meals, and raise the risk of food or drink going into the lungs.

Therapy combines exercises that strengthen and coordinate the swallowing muscles, safer swallowing techniques, and changes to food and drink textures. Where it is suitable, we also use electrical stimulation alongside the exercises to help retrain the muscles.

Signs it might help

  • Coughing or choking while eating or drinking
  • Food feeling stuck in the throat
  • A wet or gurgly voice after swallowing
  • Meals taking much longer than they used to
  • Unplanned weight loss or repeated chest infections

Families ask

Good questions.

Does electrical stimulation hurt?

Most people describe it as a tingling or tapping feeling. The intensity is adjusted to what is comfortable for you.

Can therapy start during cancer treatment?

Often, yes. Starting early can help, and we will fit the timing around your treatment.

Do you treat children with swallowing difficulties?

Yes. For babies and young children we usually start with feeding therapy, which covers swallowing too.
