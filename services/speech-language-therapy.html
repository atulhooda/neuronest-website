About this therapy

What it is.

Speech and language therapy helps with how a child understands words, puts them together and says them clearly. It covers late talking, unclear speech, stammering, and difficulty following instructions or joining a conversation.

Sessions are built around play, because children learn language best when they are enjoying themselves. You also go home with simple things to try, since most language is learned between sessions, not in them.

Signs it might help

  • Very few words by age two
  • Speech that others find hard to understand by three or four
  • Stammering or getting stuck on words
  • Difficulty following simple instructions
  • Pointing or pulling you along instead of using words

Families ask

Good questions.

Should we wait and see if my child catches up?

Some children do catch up on their own, but many don't. An early assessment does no harm, and if all is well you will know sooner.

Do you check hearing as well?

Often, yes. We can test hearing in the clinic, which rules out one of the most common causes of speech delay.

Do you see adults?

Yes, for speech and language difficulties after a stroke or brain injury, through adult neuro-communication therapy.
